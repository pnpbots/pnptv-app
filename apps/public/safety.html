Safety & Harm Reduction

Last updated: March 4, 2026

PNPtv is committed to the health and safety of our community. As a PNP community platform, we believe in harm reduction over judgment. This page provides practical safety information, resources, and guidance for our members. Your well-being matters to us.

1. Our Harm Reduction Philosophy

PNPtv takes a harm reduction approach. We recognize that members of our community make their own choices, and our role is to provide honest, non-judgmental information that helps you make those choices as safely as possible. Harm reduction means:

  • Meeting people where they are, without judgment or shame.
  • Providing accurate, evidence-based information about risks and how to reduce them.
  • Acknowledging that abstinence is one valid choice among many, and not the only measure of success.
  • Prioritizing the preservation of life and health above all else.
  • Respecting the dignity and autonomy of every community member.

2. Substance Use Safety

If you use substances, here are practical steps to reduce risks:

  • Never use alone. Always have someone you trust nearby. If you are using alone, consider using the Never Use Alone hotline (1-800-484-3731) where an operator stays on the line with you.
  • Start low, go slow. If you are trying something new or from a new source, use a small amount first to gauge its strength and your reaction.
  • Test your substances. Fentanyl test strips and reagent testing kits can identify dangerous adulterants. Many harm reduction organizations distribute free test strips.
  • Carry naloxone (Narcan). Naloxone can reverse opioid overdoses and save lives. It is available without a prescription in many areas. Learn how to use it and keep it accessible.
  • Stay hydrated. Drink water regularly, especially if you are active. But avoid overhydrating -- sip, do not chug.
  • Eat and rest. Taking care of your body's basic needs makes a significant difference. Try to eat before and after sessions, and prioritize sleep.
  • Know your interactions. Research how different substances interact with each other and with any medications you take. Some combinations are dangerous or fatal.
  • Set limits for yourself. Decide your limits before you start, and stick to them. It is harder to make good decisions once you have started.
  • Take breaks. Give your body and mind time to recover between sessions. Extended use without breaks increases health risks significantly.

3. Sexual Health

Sexual health is a critical part of community wellness. Protect yourself and your partners:

  • PrEP (Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis): PrEP is highly effective at preventing HIV. Talk to your healthcare provider about whether PrEP is right for you. Many clinics offer it at low or no cost.
  • Condoms and barriers: Use condoms, dental dams, and other barrier methods to reduce STI transmission. Keep them accessible.
  • Regular testing: Get tested for HIV and STIs regularly -- at least every 3 months if you are sexually active with multiple partners. Many cities offer free or low-cost testing.
  • U=U (Undetectable = Untransmittable): People living with HIV who maintain an undetectable viral load through treatment cannot transmit HIV sexually. Stigma has no place in our community.
  • PEP (Post-Exposure Prophylaxis): If you think you have been exposed to HIV, PEP can prevent infection if started within 72 hours. Go to an emergency room or urgent care immediately.
  • Communicate openly: Talk with your partners about boundaries, status, testing, and safer sex practices. Honest communication is the foundation of safety.
  • Consent always: Consent must be clear, ongoing, and freely given. If someone is incapacitated, they cannot consent. Check in with your partners regularly.

4. Meeting Up Safely

If you connect with someone on PNPtv and decide to meet in person, take these precautions:

  • Tell someone: Let a trusted friend know where you are going, who you are meeting, and when you expect to return. Share your location with them if possible.
  • Meet in public first: If you can, meet in a public place before going to a private location. This gives you a chance to assess the situation.
  • Trust your instincts: If something feels wrong, leave. You do not owe anyone an explanation. Your safety comes first.
  • Have your own transportation: Make sure you have a way to leave independently (your own car, rideshare app, public transit fare).
  • Keep your phone charged: Ensure your phone is charged and you have a way to call for help if needed.
  • Verify identity: If possible, verify the person's identity before meeting. Video chat on the platform first.
  • Avoid sharing your home address until you have established trust. Consider meeting at a neutral location first.
  • Set a check-in time: Arrange a check-in call or text with your trusted friend at a specific time. If they do not hear from you, they should know to follow up.

5. Mental Health

Your mental health is just as important as your physical health. The PNP lifestyle can be intense, and it is important to take care of your emotional well-being:

  • Check in with yourself. Regularly assess how you are feeling emotionally. Are you using substances or sex to cope with difficult emotions? It is okay to seek help.
  • Watch for warning signs: Isolation, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, difficulty functioning in daily life, paranoia, or prolonged depression may be signs that you need support.
  • Reach out. Talking to someone -- a friend, a counselor, a hotline -- is a sign of strength, not weakness.
  • Take breaks from the platform. Social media and online communities can be overwhelming. It is healthy to disconnect regularly.
  • Set boundaries. You are allowed to say no, to log off, to not respond, to take space. Protect your energy.

6. Crisis Resources

If you or someone you know is in crisis, please reach out to these resources:

  • Emergency Services: Call 911 (US), 123 (Colombia), or your local emergency number for immediate danger.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US): Call or text 988. Available 24/7. 988lifeline.org
  • Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 (US), 686868 (Canada), or 85258 (UK). crisistextline.org
  • The Trevor Project (LGBTQ+): Call 1-866-488-7386, text START to 678-678, or chat at thetrevorproject.org. 24/7 support for LGBTQ+ individuals.
  • Trans Lifeline: Call 877-565-8860 (US) or 877-330-6366 (Canada). translifeline.org
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: Call 1-800-662-4357. Free, confidential, 24/7 treatment referral and information. samhsa.gov
  • Never Use Alone: Call 1-800-484-3731. An operator stays on the line while you use, and calls emergency services if you become unresponsive. neverusealone.com

8. Reporting Unsafe Behavior

If you witness or experience unsafe behavior on PNPtv, report it immediately:

  • In-App: Use the report button on profiles, messages, content, or live rooms.
  • Email: Contact support@pnptv.app with details of the situation.
  • What to report: Threats, harassment, coercion, suspected underage users, non-consensual sharing of content, exploitation, or any behavior that makes you feel unsafe.
  • Confidentiality: All safety reports are handled confidentially. Your identity will not be shared with the reported user.

If someone is in immediate physical danger, always call local emergency services first.

9. Platform Safety Features

PNPtv provides several built-in safety features:

  • Block and Mute: You can block or mute any user at any time. Blocked users cannot see your profile, contact you, or interact with your content.
  • Content Reporting: One-tap reporting on all content, messages, and profiles.
  • Privacy Controls: Control who can see your profile, message you, and view your content through your privacy settings.
  • Live Room Moderation: Room hosts can remove disruptive participants and set room rules.
  • Age Verification: All users must confirm they are 18+. Creators undergo additional identity verification.

10. Community Care

We are all part of this community, and we all have a role in keeping it safe:

  • Look out for each other. If someone seems like they are struggling, check in on them.
  • Share harm reduction information openly and without judgment.
  • Carry naloxone if you can. You could save a life.
  • Respect people's boundaries, choices, and autonomy.
  • Be honest about your status, boundaries, and intentions.
  • If you are going through a difficult time, reach out. You are not alone.

Remember: Asking for help is always the right choice. There is no shame in it. We are here for each other.
